causal-mask -- why decode only attends to the past

A language model writes one word at a time, and it is never allowed to look at what comes after the position it is working on. A mask enforces that by blocking everything later in the text for every position, which leaves the familiar triangle of allowed comparisons. The rule matters most during training, where seeing even one word ahead would let the model copy the answer it is meant to guess and learn nothing. It sounds like a restriction and it is really an efficiency: because each position is blinded to its own future, an entire book can be shown to the model in one pass and still produce a fair next-word test at every one of its thousands of positions.

Interactive page: the lower-triangular causal mask, and the reason for it — an autoregressive LM predicting the next token must not peek at it. Anchor: A3 attention pattern (Family B).

What it shows

An N×N grid: rows are query positions, columns are key positions, over a real token sequence. A cell [i,j] is visible when j ≤ i masked (−∞ → 0 weight after softmax) when j > i — the lower triangle is allowed, the upper triangle is masked. Scrub the query position i: the current row lights its visible prefix (tokens 0..i), the masked future is darkened, and the readout points out that query i is predicting token i+1, which sits in the masked region — so it can't cheat by reading the answer. The key tokens on top dim as they cross into the future; the query tokens on the left mark the current position. This is the mask step of ../scaled-dot-attention/, made its own focus.
